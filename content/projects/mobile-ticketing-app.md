### [](#Overview "Overview")Overview

With more and more tasks performed on mobile nowadays, the need for a mobile solution is imperative for a business’s success. Qualtrics barely had any mobile products. For customers who needed to access our products from a smartphone, it was clearly a poor user experience. We fell short of customers’ expectations and the tech trends. It became clear that we need to invest on mobile. The Follow-up Ticketing tool had the most obvious mobile use case and it could have a direct impact on business and sales. So we decided to make our first mobile investment on follow-up ticketing app.

  

### [](#Role "Role")Role

Led the design. Collaborated with Product Managers on customer validation.

  

### [](#Strategy "Strategy")Strategy

**Understand our users:** Before doing any feature prioritization, we used our ticketing persona to understand our users in a mobile context. We mainly tried to resolve two things:

*   What are they frustrated with in our offering at the moment that could be done better via mobile?
    
*   What features would be critical for providing a mobile experience?
    

**Assess the situation:** We needed to think about whether a fully functional mobile app is really what we need – or is it possible that our users only need a small subset of functionality on the move and that they’ll do most of their work at a desktop?

**The answer is:** For our users, managing and resolving tickets is their day-to-day job. Most of the time, they are in an office environment at a desktop and this involves some complex administrative tasks and a more sophisticated ticket resolving process. However, when users aren’t physically with a desktop or have an simple but urgent task, it’s much convenient to perform on mobile. So the mobile app is not a replicate of the existing ticketing tool. Our users only need access to a subset of functionality so that they quickly accomplish some basic but important ticket resolving tasks like changing ticket status, re-assigning, leaving comments, sending emails etc.

  

### [](#Goal "Goal")Goal

We aimed to design a follow-up ticketing mobile app for our users to view and resolve tickets from their phone. Our design should respect users’ cognition of using the existing web ticketing product. Features are where users expect them to be. The learning curve and potential confusion need to be minimized.

  

### [](#Architecture "Architecture")Architecture

We prioritized what went into the mobile experience based on the desired user workflow. Both my product manager and engineers were closely involved to make sure the architecture is right and feasible.

![](/images/Mobile-Ticketing-App/Architecture.png)

  

### [](#Design "Design")Design

One of the design goals was to make features be where users expect them to be so that users feel in control and no extra understanding is required. To achieve this goal, we tried similar ways to group content and controls and used consistent visual style with the web experience.

**Home:** In order to make each ticket as visually single and contained unit, we adopted the card pattern. Ticket priority, title, status and last updated activity were identified as the most important information. They were added to each ticket to help users distinguish tickets with a qucik scan.

![](/images/Mobile-Ticketing-App/Home.png)

**Sort & Filter:** It’s important that users are able to sort and filter tickets based on their needs. Users can sort by any type of ticket data on the web app. On the mobile, we limited only sorting by created date, updated date, and priority which users can easily find out on the ticket list.

![](/images/Mobile-Ticketing-App/Sort%20&%20Filter.png)

**Ticket Details:** The ticket details follow how they were structure on the web app. All key functionalities including status, assignee, team and cause change are enabled on mobile. Users can still leave comments to the tickets.

![](/images/Mobile-Ticketing-App/Ticket%20Details.png)

**Send Email:** During the early access, we learned that Send Email function is the No.1 requested feature. We added this feature so that users can send emails within the app.

![](/images/Mobile-Ticketing-App/Send%20Email.png)  
  

### [](#Launch "Launch")Launch

The participation in the early access program has been available since early 2018. We were able to test the app, solve some usability issues and add few critical missing features. The Follow Up app was launched in June. Our team looked forward to hearing more feedback to improve. As a designer, I value carefully thought out and crafted details. At the time of launch, I already noticed some gaps between the design and what was implemented, mostly in visual treatment. This will be worked on next.
